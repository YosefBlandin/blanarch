# Blanarch fixes FortiClient's daily pains, not its future

FortiClient VPN's users mostly complain about four things. Connections drop and do not come back on their own. Connections fail without a useful error. SSO on Android only works with Chrome. Passwords and one-time codes have to be typed again and again. On paper, Blanarch fixes most of these on Android, and several fully: clear errors, SSO in any browser, a password saved in the Keystore, emailed codes read automatically, and certificate pinning. The biggest weakness is not a missing screen. It is the protocol. **FortiOS 7.6.3 (April 2025) removed SSL-VPN tunnel mode from every FortiGate and deletes the config on upgrade** ([Fortinet release notes](https://docs.fortinet.com/document/fortigate/7.6.3/fortios-release-notes/173430/ssl-vpn-tunnel-mode-no-longer-supported)). The last branch that still has it, 7.4, reaches end of support on **11 Nov 2028** ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/fortigate-3/technical-tip-fortios-end-of-support-change-for-fortios-v7-4-and-v7-6-224876)). That gives an SSL-only client about two years of supported gateways, and the pool shrinks every time a gateway upgrades. The complaints about FortiClient's IPsec client are growing fast (weak crypto options, DH-group mismatches, config that cannot be imported), and Blanarch cannot address any of them. Admin needs also stay out of reach without deep integration: host checks, EMS/ZTNA posture, and pushing settings through MDM. Every "solved" in this report is on paper only. Blanarch has not yet completed a real connection to a real gateway, so the first priority is to prove the tunnel works. The second is to make it survive drops and network changes. The third is to decide now whether Blanarch gets an IPsec/IKEv2 path.

## Dropped tunnels and silent failures top the one-star pile

The evidence base is lopsided. On Android, **FortiClient VPN has 27,581 ratings averaging 3.73 stars, and about 23% of them are 1 star**. The EMS-managed FortiClient app averages 3.29 with about 30% 1-star ([Google Play VPN](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US), [Google Play EMS](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_fa&hl=en)). iOS volume is tiny but worse: **2.2 stars from 118 US ratings** for the VPN app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/forticlient-vpn/id1475674905?see-all=reviews)). A keyword tally of 254 Android 1-2 star reviews from 2022 onward gives this rough ranking: cannot connect 17%, drops and reconnects 15%, broken by an update 12%, SSO/SAML 10%, IPsec/SSL 8%, UI 5%, OTP/2FA 5%, password saving 4%. The tally is English-only, and many of these reviews are one-liners. A manual read of 180 substantive negative reviews puts drops first, at about 40 reviews ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US)). Desktop evidence comes from Fortinet's forum, review sites and Hacker News. It adds DNS breakage, macOS upgrade waves and a poor Linux client. Reddit could not be retrieved in any research pass, so the loudest informal admin channel is missing from this picture.

The drop complaint is really three complaints. The tunnel dies. The app does not reconnect. The user is not told. One reviewer said it was "disconnected after every 5 to 10 mins", while "you've to work with vpn connected 8 to 10 hrs daily". Another asked for "a notification upon disconnect and an option to reconnect. I get disconnections all the time and I don't even realize it for a while" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US)). A policy decision makes this worse. **After FortiClient v6, auto-connect and always-up moved behind the EMS licence** ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiClient/Technical-Tip-FortiClient-licensing-and-support/ta-p/196327), [51SEC](https://blog.51sec.org/2024/11/forticlient-advanced-features-idle.html)). One desktop admin found auto-reconnect only worked once "Save Password" was enabled, "which I'm really not crazy about doing", and even then DUO MFA was required again ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/Support-Forum/SSL-VPN-Not-auto-reconnecting/td-p/242860)). The gateway defaults add scheduled drops: a **5-minute idle timeout and an 8-hour auth timeout** ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/fortigate-3/technical-tip-ssl-vpn-timers-explanation-and-ssl-vpn-login-attempt-limit-aka-lockout-102761)). On Android, the 2022 wave of Android 12 reports had the VPN dying within seconds of the app going to the background. Battery-permission demands followed, which reviewers called intrusive ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US)).

Silent failures are the second pillar. Reviewers describe "three dots and then nothing - no error msg", and "Every other update seems to break VPN functionality" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US)). On the desktop, the errors do exist but point the wrong way. **"The VPN server may be unreachable" is shown for no internet, an IP lockout, a SAML login that timed out (-6005), and even a successful login with no firewall policy behind it (-14)** ([Fortinet Community -14](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/Support-Forum/VPN-Server-may-be-unreachable-14-Forticlient-SSL-VPN/td-p/245552), [Fortinet Community -6005](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiGate/Troubleshooting-Tip-How-to-fix-the-error-Unable-to-establish-VPN/ta-p/274253)). "-7200 Credential or SSLVPN configuration is wrong" covers wrong passwords, locked AD accounts, Windows Internet Options, and gateways where the 7.6.3 upgrade quietly switched off tunnel mode ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiGate/Troubleshooting-Tip-FortiClient-error-Credential-or-SSL-VPN/ta-p/392381)). Help desks diagnose from the progress percentage, using a map that Fortinet staff published ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiGate/Troubleshooting-Tip-Possible-reasons-for-FortiClient-SSL-VPN/ta-p/211965)). The most-documented ticket driver is **"Permission denied (-455)"**. It hides username case, group and portal mapping, and MFA state behind one message ([rottenwifi](https://rottenwifi.com/fix-forticlient-ssl-vpn-permission-denied-455/)). Free-client users get no Fortinet support at all, so all of this lands on internal help desks ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiClient/Technical-Tip-FortiClient-licensing-and-support/ta-p/196327)).

## Blanarch answers the Android user's list almost line by line

The mobile complaints read like a spec for Blanarch's features, and several map across cleanly. **SSO in the default browser through Custom Tabs** directly answers the Chrome-only lock-in. That complaint shows up in 25-upvote reviews from Edge-only work profiles, and it has repeated from 2022 to 2026 ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US)). One caveat: Blanarch uses the same loopback 127.0.0.1:8020 redirect, and "127.0.0.1 refused to connect" is itself a known failure ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/Support-Forum/Failed-on-FortiClientVPN-with-SSO-SAML-MFA-using-O365-on-Android/td-p/258344)). The listener's lifecycle while the browser is open needs testing. **Errors that give a cause, a fix and technical details** answer "no error msg". Stopping at once on a rejected password answers FortiGate's default **lockout after 2 failed attempts, with a 60 s block per source IP** ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/fortigate-3/technical-tip-ssl-vpn-timers-explanation-and-ssl-vpn-login-attempt-limit-aka-lockout-102761)). **Reading emailed codes automatically over IMAP** answers the case where default email and SMS codes expire in 60 s ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiGate/Troubleshooting-Tip-SSL-VPN-and-two-factor-expiry-timers/ta-p/191661)). It also removes the app switch that makes FortiClient drop out of Recents mid-login. **Fingerprint confirmation with per-host pinning** replaces the yes/no "untrusted issuer" prompt and its global "do not warn" option, which train users to accept any certificate ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiGate/Technical-Tip-How-to-fix-FortiClient-VPN-certificate-error-The/ta-p/368678)).

The admin side matches less well, but it still matches in useful places. Setup links and QR codes fill the gap between "email a PDF of instructions" and "buy EMS plus Intune", which is where free-client organisations sit today ([Fortinet Intune guide](https://docs.fortinet.com/document/forticlient/7.4.0/intune-deployment-guide/721527/enrolling-forticlient-mobile-endpoints-to-ems-with-intune-integration)). The connected-screen details sheet shows tunnel IP, routing, DNS and sign-in method. That targets the "connected but no traffic" and split-DNS cases, where the tunnel looks up but names do not resolve. Offering always-on and reconnect for free removes a licence gate that admins can see. Two tensions deserve honesty. First, some admins turn off "allow client to save password" on purpose ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/Support-Forum/Fortigate-60F-SSL-VPN-Portal-quot-allow-client-to-save-password/td-p/248314)). A client that saves the password anyway may read to them as bypassing policy, even behind a biometric app lock. Second, a clear message cannot fix server-side causes such as a missing group mapping or firewall policy. Blanarch can say "signed in but not allowed" more precisely, but the ticket still goes to IT.

## FortiOS 7.6.3 put an expiry date on SSL-VPN clients

This is the fact that frames the product's lifespan. Fortinet removed SSL-VPN in three stages. **FortiOS 7.6.0 removed SSL VPN from models with 2 GB RAM or less** (FG-40F/60F/61F and similar) ([Fortinet 7.6.0 notes](https://docs.fortinet.com/document/fortigate/7.6.0/fortios-release-notes/877104/ssl-vpn-removed-from-2gb-ram-models-for-tunnel-and-web-mode)). **7.6.3, released 17 April 2025, removed tunnel mode on all models.** Existing tunnel config is not migrated: it is "automatically removed after upgrade" ([Fortinet migration guide](https://docs.fortinet.com/document/fortigate/7.6.3/administration-guide/155142/ssl-vpn-tunnel-mode-to-ipsec-vpn-migration-new), [boll.ch](https://blog.boll.ch/fortios-7-6-3-ssl-vpn-tunnel-mode-is-no-longer-supported/)). **On the 7.4 branch, 7.4.8 removed it from the entry 50G/70G/90G models** ([Mirazon](https://www.mirazon.com/rip-fortigate-ssl-vpn-what-you-need-to-know/)). What remains for Blanarch is larger FortiGates on 7.4.x or older, and those on 7.6.0-7.6.2. Fortinet recommends dial-up IPsec with IKEv2 over TCP 443 as the replacement ([forti.blog](https://forti.blog/guides/forticlient-dial-up-ipsec-vpn-on-fortigate)). Its stated motives, a run of exploited sslvpnd bugs and a push toward ZTNA and FortiSASE, make a reversal very unlikely ([Mirazon](https://www.mirazon.com/rip-fortigate-ssl-vpn-what-you-need-to-know/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/04/fortinet-warns-attackers-retain.html)).

The deadline that matters is **11 November 2028**, when FortiOS 7.4 support ends ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/fortigate-3/technical-tip-fortios-end-of-support-change-for-fortios-v7-4-and-v7-6-224876)). As of September 2026, that leaves about 26 months in which organisations can run supported firmware and still offer SSL-VPN. After that, the only gateways left are ones admins keep on unsupported firmware. That is exactly the population the FortiBleed leak and the SSL-VPN CVE history make risky ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fortibleed-leak-exposes-fortinet-vpn-credentials-for-73-000-devices/)). No source measured what share of deployed FortiGates are on 7.6.3+ today, so the rate of shrinkage is unknown. The direction is not. Blanarch should also expect a failure mode it can explain well. Gateways upgraded to 7.6.3 produced a wave of -7200 errors with correct credentials ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiGate/Troubleshooting-Tip-FortiClient-error-Credential-or-SSL-VPN/ta-p/392381)). A clear "this gateway no longer offers SSL-VPN tunnel mode, ask IT for the IPsec settings" message would turn a dead end into a useful answer.

The same shift creates an opening, and today Blanarch cannot take it. The IPsec complaints are the fastest-growing category. Examples: "absolutely awful since they forced ipsec only", missing AES-256 and DH groups 20/21, and "No ability to import a config file... Making sysadmins give users the PSK directly is kind of absurd" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US)). Fortinet's own KB confirms that **Android FortiClient cannot pick DH groups above 14** ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiGate/Technical-Tip-Android-Forticlient-VPN-does-not-connect-and-the/ta-p/277238)). One forum thread ties a 7.6.5 upgrade to Android failures with no fix posted ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/Support-Forum/FortiClient-VPN-Android-7-4-3-not-connecting-to-FortiGate-after/td-p/424380)). Free-client users are also unsure whether IKEv2 over TCP is supported for them at all ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/support-forum-92/dialup-ipsec-over-tcp-with-forticlient-vpn-a-gotcha-221477)). The third-party field is almost empty. openfortivpn and OpenConnect are both tied to SSL-VPN tunnel mode, and OpenConnect's Fortinet reconnect "still doesn't work very well" ([OpenConnect](https://www.infradead.org/openconnect/fortinet.html)). A clean, Fortinet-focused Android client that speaks IKEv2 with modern crypto and handles OTP well would face little competition. One risk is open: nothing in the research confirms whether Fortinet's IKEv2-over-TCP is plain RFC 8229, which decides whether Android's built-in IKEv2 or strongSwan can talk to it.

## Eight gaps, ordered by what they unlock

**1. Confirm a real end-to-end connection.** Every "solved" above depends on it, and until the Activity log shows "Connected" against a real gateway, the product has no proven value. This is the cheapest item and it blocks everything else.

**2. Make drop handling airtight.** Drops are the most common and most damaging complaint, and Blanarch is already mostly built for them. What is left is small and has high impact. Verify that the tunnel survives the app going to the background and a Wi-Fi to mobile handover (FortiClient's Android 12 and IP-change failures). Post an explicit "VPN dropped" notification with Reconnect. At the 8-hour auth timeout, re-sign in with minimal effort: fetch the new emailed code automatically, so the user only confirms. This is where Blanarch can clearly beat the free FortiClient, which has no auto-reconnect.

**3. Decide on IPsec/IKEv2 now, and start with a spec.** This is the only gap that decides whether the product exists in 2029. Under the clean-room rule the work begins in `docs/protocol/`, so the first step is a spec for Fortinet dial-up IKEv2 (TCP 443 encapsulation, EAP and OTP handling, DH groups 14/19/20/21), and a test of whether Android's `Ikev2VpnProfile` can carry it. If it cannot, the choice is a much larger engine or a deliberately time-limited product. Either way, the decision should be made on purpose.

**4. Detect and explain gateways Blanarch cannot serve.** Say plainly when tunnel mode is disabled or missing (7.6.3+), when the portal requires a host check, or when a client certificate is demanded. This cheap step turns the "not solved" rows into clear, supportable messages instead of 1-star "doesn't work" reviews. It also fits Blanarch's error design.

**5. Add Android managed configuration (app restrictions).** An MDM could then push host, port, backup addresses and pin without EMS. It costs little, reuses the setup-link data model, and it is what IT at mid-size organisations will ask for first ([Fortinet Intune guide](https://docs.fortinet.com/document/forticlient/7.4.0/intune-deployment-guide/721527/enrolling-forticlient-mobile-endpoints-to-ems-with-intune-integration)). It could also carry an admin flag such as "do not save password", which resolves the policy tension above.

**6. Client-certificate authentication through Android KeyChain.** This is a gateway requirement that a client can implement, and openfortivpn and OpenConnect already offer it ([openfortivpn](https://github.com/adrienverge/openfortivpn)). Without it, some gateways are closed to Blanarch.

**7. iOS.** iOS reviews are the harshest (2.2 stars), and "save password" is their signature complaint. The search found no third-party Fortinet client on iOS. The market is attractive, but it needs a NetworkExtension port. That makes sense only after items 1 to 3, and after the IPsec decision, since an SSL-only iOS client would inherit the same deadline.

**8. The long tail: IPv6 in the tunnel, DTLS, SMS code reading, profile backup.** Each fixes a real but narrow complaint. Profile backup is the one with a top-voted review behind it ("8 profiles gone", 56 helpful) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortinet.forticlient_vpn&hl=en_US)).

Host checks, EMS/ZTNA posture and TAC-level support are left off this list on purpose. They depend on Fortinet's agent reporting to EMS ([Fortinet Community](https://community.fortinet.com/t5/FortiClient/Technical-Tip-FortiClient-licensing-and-support/ta-p/196327)). A third-party client should say it does not support them rather than try to imitate them.

## Conclusion

The complaint data points to one clear conclusion. What FortiClient's users hate is mostly product care, not protocol. Reconnecting, saving a password, letting the user pick a browser, and naming the real error are all things Fortinet chose to license, neglect or leave broken across releases. Blanarch's design choices line up with the pain almost exactly. That is a strong sign the product is solving the right problems, and a weak sign that it works, because none of it has been proven against a gateway yet.

The strategic picture changes the question. Blanarch should not ask "how good an SSL-VPN client can we build". It should ask "what does the user carry across the move to IPsec". Settings shared by link and QR, pinned certificates, automatic OTP handling, and error messages that name the cause all work the same whatever the transport underneath. If Blanarch treats its SSL engine as the first of two transports rather than the product itself, the 7.6.3 removal becomes the moment free-client organisations go looking for an alternative, rather than an expiry date. If it stays SSL-only, it has a polished client for a market that shrinks every time a gateway upgrades, with support for the last SSL-VPN branch ending in November 2028.
